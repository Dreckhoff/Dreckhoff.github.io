Ending my Master, Starting my PhD

June 18, 2026 General 5 min read

It's done. I have finally completed my studies in Heidelberg last december by handing in my master thesis. Until now, I am employed by my group to work on a paper about my thesis, and I plan to go to ISTA in August to start my PhD..

The End of my Master

A somewhat emotional topic. Finishing your master, or any degree for that matter, is probably something of a magnitude that you only learn to appreciate much later on. Everything seems to be on a track, it is inevitable that you complete your master, that you — or at least I — never stop to think about what it took to actually arrive at that point. But besides that, I think it was quite a nice experience all in all. Of course, there were ups and downs, but if I look back, I think I would choose this path again, maybe with one or two exceptions.

On the more professional side, I am very happy about the progress I made during my master thesis. Of course I learned a lot:

  • About my specific topic, clathrin mediated endocytosis, and its role in the cell, biology and biophysics in general.
  • About working on a topic that you are initially unfamiliar with. How to sort your questions, and how to tackle them.
  • About the scientific process in general. How do you communicate your thoughts, your questions, and your results effectively and efficiently?
I have held talk about my topic (at Roche, at ISTA, and of course my group), created posters to explain my research, and have spoken with many people about my work. I think all of this really showed me that it is irrelevant how good your research is if nobody understands what you are doing. When I was designing my poster for the DPG , I sat down at some point and thought: "I would not find my poster exciting", and redesigned it completely. The feedback was so positive, that I hope I will always put as much effort into my poster (and talks and papers) as I put in that time. I also learned that no matter how easy you think a topic is, really, REALLY start at the very basics. And even if you think that everybody in the room knows this and will think you are stupid for explaining it, do it anyway, as there will be so many people to shy to ask for an explanation of basic stuff.

About the Thesis

At the end of the journy, I handed in my master thesis in december of 2025. It was on clathrin mediated endocytosis (CME), about which you can read more about here. Together with my supervisor — Leon Lettermann — I wrote a python software package called Endopythosis which simulates the process of CME, allows very detailed control of every parameter, and is quite fast. We used JAX, Googles machine learning framework, to really speed it up, and it paid off in the end. This was also a very valuable lesson, to learn a (semi) new programming language for a project. Besides the computational part, we did — as a theoretical physicist quite mandatory — some theoretical background work, that laid the foundation for the paper we wrote later. Of course, with my knowledge and experience now, the time to produce the thesis could have probably been cut in half. But I guess this is the journey one has to take, and I am happy to have done that.

Finding a PhD Position

Already while writing the master thesis, I was thinking about what to do afterwards. At that time, this question bothered not only me, but almost all of my friends. Most of us like science, like physics, and want to do research further. But as many people nowadays know, the working conditions in academia are very lacking. The same was true for me: I knew I would like to work further in physics, but I also knew that a secure job with good pay is also nothing you should reject prematurely. What I decided on was to apply for PhD positions simulatenously to applying to more "traditional" jobs in industry.

In parallel I applied for industry jobs and PhD positions. For the first, I applied to banks, investment companies, insurances, trading companies, consulting companies, and pharma companies. However, nothing worked out in the end. Though I got invited to some interviews, in the end I dont think I had the feeling I belonged there (yet). Luckily I still had the other path. And the more I thought about it, the better it sounded. Being the boss of your own time, working on topics you find interesting, speaking to lots of interested and interesting people, it does not sound too bad, right? Also, still having the option to enjoy parts of your student life seemed very attractive to me. Of course, doing a PhD is very daunting, right? You will work on a topic that is new, difficult, and unlike during the master thesis, there is no so direct supervisor anymore. But I think the longer I worked on my master thesis, the more comfortable I got and the more I trusted my own skills to tackle that challenge.

So I talked with Ulrich about where to apply. I had talsk with lots of people, e.g. with Ivan Palaia from the Imperial College and David Brückner from the BioZentrum Basel. In the end, I decided to accept an offer from Austria by the Institute of Science and Technology Austria (ISTA) . Ulrich created the contact with Andela Saric, I had a very nice talk with her, applied and in the end, got accepted. Of course I am very happy about that. The graduate school program will start in September, and I have already found an appartment

Let's see where this journey takes me. I am looking forward to it...
